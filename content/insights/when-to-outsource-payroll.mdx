In most growing companies, payroll starts as a spreadsheet looked after by someone in finance. It works, until the headcount doubles, a state is added, or the person who understands the spreadsheet goes on leave.

Here are the signs we see most often in companies that are ready to hand it over.

## 1. The last week of the month disappears

If your finance or HR lead spends three or four days every month on payroll inputs, checks and corrections, that's time not spent on work only they can do. Outsourcing doesn't remove their oversight. It removes the manual grind.

## 2. You've had a compliance scare

A late PF payment, a missed Professional Tax filing in a new state, a TDS notice. Even small penalties are a signal that the calendar is too complicated to manage alongside everything else.

## 3. Employees keep asking about their payslips

When the same questions about deductions, tax and reimbursements land on one person's desk every month, it's a sign the process isn't transparent enough, and that the person answering them is stretched.

## 4. You're growing into new states or adding contract staff

Each new state brings its own Professional Tax and labour rules. Contract staff add another layer. This is usually the moment spreadsheets stop scaling.

## 5. Only one person really knows how it works

If payroll depends on a single person's memory, it's a risk, however good they are. A documented, outsourced process with maker-checker review protects you and them.

## What a good switch looks like

A careful payroll partner will:

- Review your current structures, policies and filings first
- Run at least one month in parallel with your existing process
- Ask for your approval on every draft before money moves
- Share registers, challans and reports after every cycle

That parallel month matters. It's how you know the numbers are right before you rely on them.
